General Age-Related Degeneration

What is General Age-Related Degeneration?

General Age-Related Degeneration refers to the gradual decline in the body's ability to repair, regenerate, and maintain normal function as a person ages. This natural process affects nearly every organ system and is associated with decreased cellular repair, chronic low-grade inflammation, reduced stem cell activity, hormonal changes, and the accumulation of cellular damage over time.

As people age, tissues and organs lose their regenerative capacity, which can result in reduced physical strength, slower recovery from injuries, impaired cognitive function, weaker immunity, and an increased risk of chronic diseases.

Although aging cannot be stopped, regenerative medicine aims to support the body's natural healing mechanisms and improve overall health and quality of life.

How It Works

Regenerative medicine for age-related degeneration focuses on enhancing the body's repair processes using advanced cellular therapies and supportive treatments. Depending on the patient's condition and clinical evaluation, therapies may include:

Mesenchymal Stem Cell (MSC) Therapy

MSCs are multipotent cells that may help modulate inflammation, support tissue repair, and promote healing by releasing bioactive molecules. They can interact with damaged tissues and contribute to regenerative processes.

Exosome Therapy

Exosomes are extracellular vesicles released by cells that carry proteins, lipids, and genetic material involved in cell-to-cell communication. They are being studied for their potential role in supporting tissue repair and reducing inflammation.

Organ-Specific Progenitor Cell Therapy

Progenitor cells are more specialized than stem cells and are intended to target specific tissues. Depending on the clinical protocol, they may be used to support regeneration in selected organs.

Immune Modulation

Some regenerative approaches aim to optimize immune function, helping regulate chronic inflammation associated with aging.

Supportive Therapies

Additional therapies may include nutritional optimization, IV vitamin infusions, physical rehabilitation, lifestyle counseling, and wellness programs tailored to the patient's needs.

The choice of therapy depends on the patient's diagnosis, medical history, and physician assessment.

Indications

General age-related degeneration therapies may be considered for individuals experiencing:

  • Reduced energy and persistent fatigue
  • Muscle weakness or loss of muscle mass
  • Joint stiffness or chronic musculoskeletal discomfort
  • Slower recovery from illness or injury
  • Decreased mobility and balance
  • Mild cognitive decline or memory concerns
  • Reduced immune function
  • Skin aging and reduced elasticity
  • Frailty associated with aging
  • General decline in physical performance
  • Reduced exercise tolerance
  • Declining quality of life related to aging

Treatment decisions should always be based on a comprehensive medical evaluation.

Contraindications

Regenerative therapies may not be appropriate for everyone. Contraindications or situations requiring caution may include:

  • Active severe infection
  • Uncontrolled systemic illness
  • Certain active cancers (depending on the therapy)
  • Pregnancy or breastfeeding (for many regenerative procedures)
  • Severe bleeding disorders or uncontrolled coagulation disorders
  • Uncontrolled autoimmune disease (depending on the therapy)
  • Recent major surgery without adequate recovery
  • Severe uncontrolled heart failure or unstable cardiovascular disease
  • Known allergy to treatment components
  • Inability to undergo the required medical procedures
  • Any condition that the treating physician determines makes therapy unsuitable

Each patient should undergo a detailed medical assessment before treatment.

Brain, Spinal Cord and Neurodegenerative Diseases

What are Brain, Spinal Cord and Neurodegenerative Diseases?

Brain, spinal cord, and neurodegenerative diseases are disorders that affect the central and peripheral nervous systems, leading to progressive loss of nerve cells (neurons), impaired communication between the brain and body, and declining neurological function.

These conditions may develop due to aging, genetic factors, autoimmune disorders, trauma, infections, vascular diseases, or unknown causes. Many neurodegenerative diseases are chronic and progressive, affecting movement, memory, cognition, sensation, speech, and daily functioning.

Regenerative medicine aims to support damaged nervous tissue, reduce inflammation, modulate immune responses, and promote tissue repair. While these therapies may help improve function or quality of life in some patients, they are not established cures for most neurodegenerative diseases.

How It Works

Regenerative medicine for neurological disorders focuses on supporting the body's natural repair mechanisms through advanced cellular therapies. Treatment is individualized based on the patient's diagnosis, disease stage, and overall health.

1. Mesenchymal Stem Cell (MSC) Therapy

MSCs are multipotent cells with immunomodulatory and anti-inflammatory properties. They may:

  • Reduce neuroinflammation
  • Promote nerve tissue repair
  • Release neurotrophic (growth) factors
  • Support damaged neurons
  • Encourage blood vessel formation (angiogenesis)
  • Improve communication between nerve cells

2. Exosome Therapy

Exosomes are tiny extracellular vesicles released by stem cells that contain proteins, lipids, RNA, and growth factors. Potential roles include:

  • Enhancing cell-to-cell communication
  • Reducing inflammation
  • Supporting neuronal survival
  • Promoting tissue regeneration
  • Protecting neurons from oxidative stress

3. Organ-Specific Progenitor Cell Therapy

Progenitor cells are specialized precursor cells that may help regenerate specific nervous tissues and support repair mechanisms within the brain or spinal cord.

4. Immune Modulation

Many neurological diseases involve chronic inflammation or immune dysfunction. Cellular therapies may help:

  • Regulate abnormal immune responses
  • Reduce inflammatory cytokines
  • Protect healthy nerve tissue
  • Slow inflammatory damage

5. Neurotrophic Growth Factor Support

Growth factors released by regenerative cells may support:

  • Neuron survival
  • Axonal regeneration
  • Synapse formation
  • Neural plasticity
  • Functional recovery

6. Rehabilitation Integration

Treatment is often combined with:

  • Physical therapy
  • Occupational therapy
  • Speech therapy
  • Cognitive rehabilitation
  • Balance and gait training

These therapies work together to maximize functional improvement.

Heart, Vessels and Cardiovascular Diseases

What are Heart, Vessels and Cardiovascular Diseases?

Heart, Vessels, and Cardiovascular Diseases (CVDs) are a group of disorders that affect the heart and blood vessels, impairing the body's ability to circulate blood efficiently. These conditions may involve damage to the heart muscle, heart valves, coronary arteries, veins, or other blood vessels and can lead to reduced oxygen delivery to vital organs.

Cardiovascular diseases are among the leading causes of illness and death worldwide. Common risk factors include aging, high blood pressure, diabetes, high cholesterol, smoking, obesity, sedentary lifestyle, and genetic predisposition.

Regenerative medicine aims to support the repair of damaged heart tissue, promote blood vessel regeneration, improve circulation, reduce inflammation, and enhance overall cardiovascular function. While promising research is ongoing, these therapies are not established cures for most cardiovascular diseases.

How It Works

Regenerative medicine for cardiovascular diseases focuses on enhancing the body's natural healing and repair mechanisms through advanced cellular therapies. Treatment is tailored to each patient's diagnosis, disease severity, and overall health.

  • Support repair of damaged heart muscle (myocardium)

  • Improve blood flow
  • Decrease scar tissue formation after heart injury
  • Support cardiac function

2. Exosome Therapy

Exosomes are tiny extracellular vesicles released by cells that carry proteins, RNA, and growth factors involved in cell communication. Potential benefits include:

  • Enhancing tissue repair
  • Supporting heart muscle recovery
  • Reducing inflammation

  • Promoting angiogenesis

3. Organ-Specific Progenitor Cell Therapy

4. Immune Modulation

Chronic inflammation contributes to many cardiovascular diseases. Cellular therapies may help regulate inflammatory responses and protect healthy cardiovascular tissues.

5. Angiogenesis Promotion

Certain regenerative therapies aim to stimulate the development of new blood vessels, potentially improving blood supply to areas affected by reduced circulation.

Digestive Tracts and Gastrointestinal Diseases

What are Digestive Tracts and Gastrointestinal Diseases?

Digestive Tract and Gastrointestinal (GI) Diseases are a group of disorders that affect the organs responsible for digesting food, absorbing nutrients, and eliminating waste. The gastrointestinal tract includes the esophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine (colon), rectum, and anus, while the digestive system also involves organs such as the liver, pancreas, and gallbladder that support digestion.

Cellular Immunotherapies: CAR-T and NK-T

What are Cellular Immunotherapies (CAR-T and NK Cell Therapy)?

Cellular Immunotherapy is an advanced form of precision medicine that uses the body's own immune cells—or specially prepared donor immune cells—to recognize and destroy cancer cells or other abnormal cells. Unlike conventional treatments such as chemotherapy or radiation, cellular immunotherapy enhances the immune system's natural ability to identify and eliminate disease.

Two of the most advanced forms of cellular immunotherapy are:

  • CAR-T Cell Therapy (Chimeric Antigen Receptor T-Cell Therapy)
  • NK Cell Therapy (Natural Killer Cell Therapy)

These therapies are primarily used in hematologic (blood) cancers, and research is ongoing for their use in solid tumors and other diseases.

Important: CAR-T therapy is an established treatment for certain blood cancers in many countries. NK cell therapies are promising but remain investigational for many indications.

How It Works

1. CAR-T Cell Therapy

CAR-T therapy is a personalized treatment in which a patient's own T lymphocytes (T cells) are collected from the blood and genetically modified in a specialized laboratory to produce Chimeric Antigen Receptors (CARs) on their surface.

These engineered receptors enable T cells to recognize specific proteins (antigens) on cancer cells. The treatment process generally involves:

  • Step 1 — Cell Collection (Leukapheresis): T cells are collected from the patient's bloodstream through a procedure called leukapheresis.
  • Step 2 — Laboratory Engineering: The collected T cells are genetically modified to express CAR receptors that specifically recognize cancer cells.
  • Step 3 — Cell Expansion: The engineered CAR-T cells are multiplied in the laboratory to produce millions of therapeutic cells.
  • Step 4 — Lymphodepleting Chemotherapy: Before infusion, patients usually receive short-course chemotherapy to prepare the immune system for the CAR-T cells.
  • Step 5 — CAR-T Cell Infusion: The engineered CAR-T cells are infused back into the patient's bloodstream.
  • Step 6 — Cancer Cell Recognition and Destruction: The CAR-T cells circulate throughout the body, identify cancer cells carrying the target antigen, attack them, multiply further, and continue destroying cancer cells.

2. NK Cell Therapy

Natural Killer (NK) cells are a type of immune cell that naturally recognize and destroy abnormal cells without prior sensitization. NK cell therapy may involve:

  • Autologous NK cells (patient's own cells)
  • Allogeneic NK cells (healthy donor cells)
  • Expanded NK cells
  • Activated NK cells
  • CAR-NK cells (genetically engineered NK cells)

Potential benefits include:

  • Recognition of cancer cells
  • Destruction of abnormal cells
  • Support of the body's immune defenses
  • Potential lower risk of graft-versus-host disease compared with some other cellular therapies (depending on the product)

3. Immune Activation

Both CAR-T and NK cell therapies work by:

  • Enhancing immune cell activity
  • Targeting abnormal cells
  • Promoting immune surveillance
  • Improving immune recognition of cancer cells

4. Precision Targeting

Unlike conventional chemotherapy, cellular immunotherapy is designed to target specific cancer-associated proteins while minimizing damage to normal tissues. However, side effects can still occur and may be serious.
